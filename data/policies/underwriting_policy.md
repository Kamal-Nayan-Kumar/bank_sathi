# How we assess an application

Every recommendation starts from the same screen, applied before we look at a
single card. It exists so that a customer who is not ready for credit is told
so clearly, instead of being shown eight cards and left to work out why none of
them would materialise.

## What we check first

- Whether you fall inside our age range.
- Whether your credit file is one we can assess at all.
- Whether there are missed payments on file.
- Whether you have been applying to a lot of cards in a short window.
- Whether your card usage is revolving heavily.

Failing any of these does not mean you are a bad risk. It means we would be
assessing you on a file that does not yet support a decision, and we would
rather say that than guess.

## Why payment history carries the most weight

A missed payment stays on your file for years, and it is the heaviest factor in
a score. It is also the most fixable in the sense that it stops getting worse
the moment autopay is set up. Nothing else you can do moves a score as quickly
or as reliably.

## Why we look at recent applications

Several applications in a short window read as financial stress to lenders, and
we hold that reading. It is a rule about the pattern, not a judgement about any
one application. A card you genuinely want, applied for once, is better for you
than three you did not think about.

## Why we look at how much of your limit you use

Revolving a balance means your usage stays high, and sustained high usage is one
of the strongest signals that a balance is not being cleared. Paying the
statement balance in full each month keeps usage low and costs you nothing. If
you are already doing that, this will not be an obstacle.

## If you have no credit history at all

That is a normal starting position, not a black mark. We will only point you at
starter and secured cards, and we will say that plainly rather than showing you
a list you cannot open.

Roughly a year of on-time payments on one card is usually enough to move up a
tier. There is no shortcut to it, and we would rather tell you that than sell
you something you cannot keep.
