# Improving your credit profile

Generic guidance, not advice about your specific situation. Nothing here is a
substitute for a conversation with a lender.

## Pay on time, every time

This is the one that matters. Payment history carries the heaviest weight in a
score, a single missed payment stays on your file for years, and autopay for
the minimum due amount removes the problem permanently.

Set it up on every card, including the one in a drawer.

## Clear the balance, do not revolve it

Card usage is the share of your limit you are using. Revolving a balance keeps
it high, and sustained high usage is a signal that a balance is not being
cleared.

Paying the statement balance in full each month costs you nothing in interest
and keeps usage low. This is the single highest-return habit change available.

## Space out applications

Every application leaves an enquiry on your file. Several at once reads as
financial stress, to us and to other lenders. A card you chose deliberately,
applied for once, beats three you did not think about.

## Do not close old cards you use

It shortens your credit history, which is part of what a score rewards. Having
an older card you actually use is an advantage, not clutter.

## If you have no history

One secured or starter card, used lightly, cleared in full every month, for
about a year. That is the route. It is slow, and there is no way around it.

## What does not work

- Taking a personal loan to move your score. It adds a new obligation and is
  usually counterproductive.
- Applying broadly across many banks in one week.
- Closing your oldest card to "clean up".
- Closing and reopening cards to reset a usage figure.
